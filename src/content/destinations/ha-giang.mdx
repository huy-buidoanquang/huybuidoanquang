Every Ha Giang Loop begins the same way: luggage strapped tight to rental motorbikes, yellow helmets buckled in the cool morning air, and a fresh red ink stamp pressed onto page one at **Kilometre Zero**.

## Kilometre Zero & the Loop Passport

Before the road ever climbs into limestone country, you stop beside National Highway 2's black stone milestone in Ha Giang City. Collecting the first stamp on a brocade-draped counter makes the loop feel less like a weekend ride and more like crossing into a republic of stone and clouds.

## Heaven's Gate & the Lonely Tree of Can Ty

Past Quan Ba Heaven's Gate, the switchbacks open over tiered valleys dusted with lingering March peach blossoms (`hoa đào`). Further along the sheer ridge of Can Ty, a single ancient tree rises straight out of the rock edge — dwarfing every rider who pauses beneath its roots.

## Tham Ma's serpent bends & the stone plateau

Then comes **Doc Tham Ma** — the unmistakable S-curve hairpin slope carved tightly down a misty gorge. A quick mug of hot coffee under timber eaves, a crimson flag snapping above the bends, a carved wooden gate welcoming bikes onto the Dong Van Karst Plateau, and lone pink peach flowers blooming stubbornly straight out of grey limestone.

## Night in Dong Van, raincoats to Lung Cu

By nightfall, Dong Van Old Town glowed amber against sheer black rock walls, warming tired hands over highland hotpot. By morning, dense mountain rain wrapped every ridge in white cloud — so the entire convoy zipped into matching burgundy raincoats and climbed the red-flag stone steps straight into the fog of **Lung Cu**, Vietnam's northernmost point.

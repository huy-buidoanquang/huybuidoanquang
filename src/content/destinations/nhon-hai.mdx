Before the heat of an August morning wakes the fishing village of Nhon Hai, the coast belongs entirely to wind, dark granite, and the slow shift from indigo into fire.

## Waiting on the headland

Climbing out across the weathered boulders before five in the morning turns the ocean into a wide-open theater. First comes a bruised violet strip above the water, then brushed streaks of coral, and finally a single blinding ember lifting straight out of the sea.

## Granite cliffs & clear tide pools

Once the sun clears the horizon, the silhouettes give way to texture: rose-tinted granite slabs carved smooth by salt spray, emerald-blue pockets of water trapped between boulders, and an entire rocky shoreline built for climbing barefoot.

## Along the rocky bay

The best hours in Nhon Hai aren't rushed from landmark to landmark — they happen perched together on warm rock ledges, listening to waves fold into the coves while the light turns from amber to crisp coastal blue.

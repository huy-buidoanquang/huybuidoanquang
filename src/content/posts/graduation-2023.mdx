Years of lectures, late-night labs, and quiet sprints toward deadlines somehow compress into a single overcast Tuesday morning in late August.

## Morning at the auditorium

The ceremony at the Academy of Cryptography Techniques opened with the familiar hum of full halls, rows of navy-and-crimson robes, and names read out one by one beneath the bright stage lights. Stepping up onto the platform to receive the red engineering diploma cover lasted only a few seconds — long enough for a handshake, a quick photo, and the quiet realization that a whole chapter had actually closed.

## Navy, crimson, and open air

Once the auditorium emptied out onto the turf field, the formality melted away immediately. The August sky stayed cool and silver over Hanoi — ideal light for pausing, catching our breath, and finally opening the crimson folder to see the degree printed in ink.

## Out on the campus track

What makes a university feel real isn't the syllabus; it's the people who walked the same corridors with you. Along the red running track and beneath the main academic tower, every corner turned into a spontaneous reunion — bouquets traded between hands, matching robes side by side, and laughs that felt like they didn't want the afternoon to end.

## Back at the desk

By the time the robes came off and the noise faded back into an ordinary Hanoi evening, two keepsakes stayed behind at home: a jar of sunflowers catching the quiet light on the stairs, and a freshly awarded red *Bằng Kỹ Sư* — which immediately proved its practical value on the desk as the world's most overqualified mousepad.

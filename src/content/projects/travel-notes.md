Places don't feel interchangeable, so the pages that hold their photographs shouldn't look identical either.

## Letting the photographs set the palette

Before laying out a travel story, the photographs are measured for dominant tone, aspect ratio, and breathing room. A misty highland morning calls for generous serif typography and open margins; an evening walk under lantern light asks for higher contrast, tighter pacing, and paired close-ups.

## Reading without friction

Every inline frame in an MDX story opens inside a keyboard-friendly lightbox with left/right navigation and crisp captions, while images below the fold load lazily so slow connections still feel effortless.
